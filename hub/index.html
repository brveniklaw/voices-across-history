Primary Sources

The Documents That Founded a Nation

1776
The Declaration of Independence
Jefferson's assertion of natural rights and the colonies' formal separation from British rule — the founding statement of American purpose.
National Archives → Library of Congress → Founders Online →
1787
The Constitution of the United States
The supreme law of the land, establishing the framework of federal government and the separation of powers among three branches.
National Archives → Congress.gov → Annotated →
1791
The Bill of Rights
The first ten amendments guaranteeing individual liberties — freedom of speech, religion, press, and the protections against government overreach.
National Archives → Library of Congress →
1787–1788
The Federalist Papers
85 essays by Hamilton, Madison, and Jay advocating for ratification of the Constitution — the definitive commentary on its meaning and intent.
Full Text (LoC) → Yale Avalon → Founders Online →
1776
Common Sense — Thomas Paine
The pamphlet that ignited popular support for independence, selling 500,000 copies in a nation of 2.5 million people.
Library of Congress → Yale Avalon →
1796
Washington's Farewell Address
Washington's warning against partisan division, entangling foreign alliances, and the dangers of regional factionalism — as relevant today as in 1796.
National Archives → U.S. Senate →
